Citations you can check

20 August 2026 · 2 min read

An LLM answers a legal question and cites an article of the law. It sounds confident. The problem is that the article can be invented: the right format, the right tone, a norm that does not exist. For legal work that is a disqualifying flaw.

In praxis the default answer is extractive: it is the text of the law itself, not the model's paraphrase. It cannot hallucinate, because it generates nothing — it quotes. A separate step checks every citation: an NLI model decides whether the norm actually supports the claim, not just resembles it in words. A reference that sounds plausible but is off-point is cut, not sent to the user.

Retrieval is hybrid: lexical plus dense, with a cross-encoder reranker on top. Quality is not asserted, it is measured on a golden set: recall@5 0.92, MRR 0.94, recomputed on every run.

An optional LLM layer exists — it makes the answer easier to read. But it passes the same citation check as the extractive one. Here the model is a convenience, not the source of truth.

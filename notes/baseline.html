A baseline, not a leaderboard

20 August 2026 · 2 min read

An RL agent is almost always compared to something. Usually to another agent on a leaderboard: PPO against SAC, +12% on Atari. For research that is fine. For an operational problem it is almost useless.

An inventory problem already has a base solution: the base-stock policy. It is decades old, it fits in two lines, and on stationary demand it is optimal. If your RL loses to base-stock, you have built a complicated way to do worse than a calculator. If it wins by one percent, the gap drowns in a single week of demand noise.

So in decisionrl every applied environment ships with its classical operations-research baseline: base-stock for inventory, a threshold rule for the queue, a greedy policy for the battery. A policy is measured against it, not asserted to be good.

An "indistinguishable" verdict is a result, not a failure. It means the agent recovered a solution that is already known, and paying for a neural network buys nothing. The interesting case is the other one — where the classical method breaks. Nonstationary demand, coupled price and order, a stockout penalty: there is no closed form, and RL starts to win for a reason you can name.

The rule is short: compute the baseline first, then train the agent. With nothing to compare against, you do not know whether it works.
